<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 0 · Sun, May 14</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>YVR → YUL → CDG → FLR</p>
</blockquote>

<p>departed Vancouver [YVR] on AC302 at 8:00am. connected in Montreal [YUL] on AF343 to Paris [CDG], then AF1066 to <strong>Florence</strong> [FLR] — overnight.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 1 · Mon, May 15</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Florence</p>
</blockquote>

<p>landed FLR on time at 9:15am — but Air France failed to transfer my checked bag. filed a lost luggage report and took a taxi to hotel. Plaza Hotel Lucchesi let me check in that early (10:30am) without question. upgraded to a top floor room with an open balcony — views of Duomo and Florence's orange-roofed skyline. with no luggage, spent an hour shopping for essentials — some clothes, charging cables, toiletries. luggage was eventually delivered to hotel the next day. Air France refunded the cost of essentials. later walked out to explore Florence. visited <strong>Basilica di Santa Croce</strong> — iconic Franciscan church, burial place of Michelangelo, Galileo, and Machiavelli. walked across <strong>Ponte Vecchio</strong> — iconic medieval bridge, above <strong>Arno river</strong>, lined with jewelry shops, built in 1345 - only Florentine bridge that survived WWII. crossed <strong>Ponte Santa Trinità</strong> — Florence's most elegant Renaissance bridge. grabbed a panino at <strong>Panini e Vini Come Dio</strong> — tiny, unassuming spot with a line out the door. simple, fresh, outstanding. visited <strong>Piazza della Signoria</strong> — main civic square, home of replica David and Loggia dei Lanzi — open-air arcade of Renaissance sculptures including Giambologna's <em>Rape of the Sabine Women</em> and Cellini's <em>Perseus</em>. completely flooded with tourists. visited <strong>Palazzo Vecchio</strong> — 13th-century fortress-palace and town hall, and climbed its <strong>Arnolfo Tower</strong> (223 steps) — best panoramic view of Florence, with Duomo filling the frame. in the evening, dinner at hotel's rooftop tapas bar — panoramic views over Florence. then walked to <strong>Piazzale Michelangelo</strong> — a hilltop terrace on south bank of Arno, famous for sunset views over the city. packed with people — waited for a gap to move in front. sunset was breathtaking. Arno glowed orange, silhouettes of bridges against the light, orange rooftops vibrating in warm glow. stayed an hour as crowd thinned — watched the city light up gradually. walked back to hotel.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Plaza Hotel Lucchesi [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Florence-Hotels-Plaza-Hotel-Lucchesi.h8741.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Florence-Hotels-Plaza-Hotel-Lucchesi.h8741.Hotel-Information</a>] — great location, everything walkable. friendly and helpful staff. early check-in allowed. top floor with open balcony — views of Duomo and Florence's skyline. rooftop tapas bar and pool with 360° views over Arno river. 5/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 2 · Tue, May 16</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Florence</p>
</blockquote>

<p>stepped out at 6am — before the city wakes up. ideal for my photography — empty streets, soft morning light, and flocks of pigeons. retraced yesterday's route to photograph: <strong>Basilica di Santa Croce</strong>, <strong>Piazza della Signoria</strong>, <strong>Ponte Vecchio</strong>, <strong>Ponte Santa Trinità</strong>. continued to <strong>Piazza del Duomo</strong> — photographed <strong>Cattedrale di Santa Maria del Fiore</strong> (known as the <strong>Duomo</strong> — symbolic cathedral of Florence, built over 140 years from 1296 to 1436, stunning facade clad in white, green and pink Tuscan marble), <strong>Campanile di Giotto</strong> (elegant 14th-century bell tower - built with same marble style as the Duomo, 414 steps), and <strong>Battistero di San Giovanni</strong> (octagonal baptistery famous for Ghiberti's gilded bronze <em>Gates of Paradise</em>). <strong>Piazza di San Lorenzo</strong> and <strong>Basilica di San Lorenzo</strong> — historic church built by Brunelleschi for the Medici family. <strong>Piazza di Santa Maria Novella</strong> and <strong>Basilica di Santa Maria Novella</strong> — iconic Gothic church with striking marble facade. <strong>Piazza Ognissanti</strong> — quiet square where Botticelli is buried. back to hotel for breakfast. at 9am — attended pre-booked skip-the-line Brunelleschi's Dome climb inside the Duomo. engineering marvel — largest brick dome ever constructed, built without scaffolding - using a double-shell design visible as you climb between two shells. views from top were unique — unlike other vantage points, you won't see the Duomo here since you're standing on it. spent the afternoon shopping — bought a Braccialini bag as a gift for my wife. dinner at Ristorante Il Paiolo — reservation recommended.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 3 · Wed, May 17</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Florence → San Gimignano → Volterra → Pienza</p>
</blockquote>

<p>good breakfast and checked out. picked up rental car — Fiat Panda — from Sicily by Car [<a href="https://www.sbc.it">https://www.sbc.it</a>], walkable from hotel. on the road by ~10am. drove south for ~2 hours to <strong>San Gimignano</strong> - a small medieval hilltop walled town famous for its 14 surviving towers, once there were 72. UNESCO World Heritage Site. parked outside walls and explored on foot — not crowded compared to Florence. visited <strong>Chiesa di Sant'Agostino</strong> (imposing 13th-century Romanesque-Gothic church), <strong>Duomo di San Gimignano</strong> (<strong>Collegiata di Santa Maria Assunta</strong> — 10th-century Romanesque church). climbed <strong>Torre Grossa</strong> — tallest surviving tower, 218 steps — panoramic 360° views over Tuscan countryside. left around 1:30pm. stopped at a panoramic viewpoint near Abbazia di Monteoliveto Minore — just 2 minutes from parking — for a classic distant shot of San Gimignano with towers rising above Tuscan valley. drove ~45 mins to <strong>Volterra</strong> — a small hilltop walled town, older and quieter than San Gimignano. parked outside, explored on foot. <strong>Piazza dei Priori</strong> — Tuscany's oldest town square. <strong>Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta</strong> — Romanesque cathedral. left around 3:45pm. drove south on the scenic <strong>Autostrada Firenze-Siena</strong>, cutting through beautiful Tuscan landscapes. stopped to photograph iconic driveway of <strong>Agriturismo Baccoleno</strong> in <strong>Crete Senesi</strong> — a long curved avenue of tall cypress trees leading to a Tuscan farmhouse. detoured to photograph <strong>Cipressi di San Quirico d'Orcia</strong> — a famous circular cluster of cypress trees standing alone in an open <strong>Val d'Orcia</strong> field. later checked into Hotel Corsignano in <strong>Pienza</strong> around 7:45pm. walked to <strong>Ristorante La Piazzetta</strong> for dinner — classic Tuscan <strong>ribollita</strong> (hearty bread and vegetable soup, slow-cooked and reheated) and a glass of <strong>Chianti</strong>.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Hotel Corsignano [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Pienza-Hotels-Hotel-Corsignano.h2525690.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Pienza-Hotels-Hotel-Corsignano.h2525690.Hotel-Information</a>] — nice location and friendly staff. parking can be an issue on a busy day. breakfast was OK. 4/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 4 · Thu, May 18</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Pienza → Montepulciano → Siena → Pienza</p>
</blockquote>

<p>breakfast at 7:30am. walked around Pienza - UNESCO World Heritage Site. <strong>Piazza Pio II</strong> (15th-century Renaissance square) and <strong>Cattedrale dell'Assunta</strong> (Pienza's Duomo, 15th-century Renaissance cathedral). later drove to <strong>Cappella della Madonna di Vitaleta</strong> — a tiny isolated chapel on a cypress-lined hilltop in Val d'Orcia. continued to <strong>Montepulciano</strong> — a hilltop Renaissance town famous for its world-class wine <strong>Vino Nobile</strong>. visited <strong>Tempio di San Biagio</strong> — stunning 16th-century Renaissance church, <strong>Piazza Grande</strong> - beautiful town square, and <strong>Palazzo Comunale</strong> - 14th-century Gothic town hall. drove to <strong>Siena</strong> — medieval city, UNESCO World Heritage Site. bigger and busier than other Tuscan hill towns but far less crowded than Florence. explored <strong>Piazza del Duomo</strong>, <strong>Duomo di Siena</strong> (iconic Gothic cathedral with a dramatic black and white striped marble facade), and <strong>Piazza del Campo</strong> — magnificent shell-shaped medieval square. quick pizza slice while walking. left after a couple of hours. drove back to Cipressi di San Quirico d'Orcia for a second attempt at the cypress circle shot — overcast, no luck. back to hotel. dinner at <strong>Baccano Panino Toscano</strong> — ribollita again with a glass of Chianti. strolled around empty streets of Pienza at night — peaceful.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 5 · Fri, May 19</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Pienza → Bologna → Modena</p>
</blockquote>

<p>checked out ~8am after a good breakfast. drove north on <strong>A1 Autostrada del Sole</strong> — perfect for long open drives. dropped off rental car in Bologna - took a taxi to <strong>Modena</strong>. checked into Best Western Milano Palace Modena by noon. in the afternoon, took a taxi to <strong>Maranello</strong> for the highlight of the trip: <strong>Ferrari factory and museum tour</strong>. the factory tour was arranged in advance through Ferrari of Seattle [<a href="https://ferrariofseattle.com/">https://ferrariofseattle.com/</a>] — not something you can just walk into. Maranello factory is where every Ferrari is hand-assembled. seeing the production line up close — including my own car being assembled — is unlike anything else. after factory, visited <strong>Museo Enzo Ferrari</strong> [<a href="https://www.museomaranello.ferrari.com/en">https://www.museomaranello.ferrari.com/en</a>] — a dreamspace for car lovers. in the evening, explored Modena: <strong>Piazza Roma</strong> (main civic square with Palazzo Ducale), <strong>Duomo di Modena</strong> (a UNESCO World Heritage Romanesque cathedral), and <strong>Via Coltellini</strong> — the umbrella street, famously decorated with hundreds of colorful umbrellas strung overhead. dinner at <strong>Stile Libero</strong>. finished with <strong>cannoli</strong> from <strong>Gioelia Cremerie</strong> — a perfect Modena night.</p>

<p><em><strong>background story</strong>: originally planned to stay in Modena through May 22 for <strong>Imola F1 Grand Prix</strong>. unfortunately it got cancelled due to severe flooding in Emilia-Romagna, a regional disaster that displaced thousands. changed plan to stay in Modena just for a night - for Ferrari factory tour. and added Cinque Terre to the itinerary. cancelled train tickets. booked new hotel. booked new rental car.</em></p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 6 · Sat, May 20</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Modena → Pisa → Massa</p>
</blockquote>

<p>breakfast at 8am. picked up second rental car — DR DR4 1.5 MT — from Drivalia [<a href="https://www.drivalia.it">https://www.drivalia.it</a>]. drove west on <strong>A1</strong>, then toward <strong>Pisa</strong>. arrived around 1:30pm — completely overrun with tourists. <strong>Piazza del Duomo</strong> (also called <strong>Piazza dei Miracoli</strong>) — UNESCO World Heritage Site — with <strong>Cattedrale di Pisa</strong> (magnificent 11th-century Romanesque cathedral), <strong>Pisa Baptistery</strong> (12th-century Romanesque baptistery) and iconic <strong>Leaning Tower</strong>. everyone doing the same tired pushing pose. street vendors and touts everywhere — persistent and hard to shake off. quickly left Piazza dei Miracoli — walked around other parts of the city instead. lunch at <strong>Ristorante India</strong> — good food. later drove north along the coast to <strong>Massa</strong> - skipped <strong>Lucca</strong> despite having planned for it, overcrowded Pisa took energy away. checked into Hotel Excelsior by 4pm. in the evening, walked the marina. fresh crepes at <strong>Yogorino</strong>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>day 7 · Sun, May 21</strong></h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Massa → Cinque Terre → Massa</p>
</blockquote>

<p>after breakfast, drove to <strong>La Spezia</strong> and parked in a pre-booked private garage - near train station. bought a <strong>Cinque Terre</strong> National Park train pass and hopped between all five villages. <strong>Riomaggiore</strong> — southernmost village. colorful stacked houses cascading down to a tiny harbor - very beautiful. <strong>Manarola</strong> — most photogenic, houses stacked above water in layers of color. <strong>Corniglia</strong> — only inland village, perched high on a cliff. had a chilled beer after climbing 382 steps on that hot day. <strong>Vernazza</strong> — charming with a natural harbor. <strong>Monterosso al Mare</strong> — largest, beach-resort-like with a proper sandy beach. back to Riomaggiore for a scheduled private boat tour with <strong>Cinque Terre Adventure</strong> [<a href="https://www.cinqueterreadventure.com">https://www.cinqueterreadventure.com</a>] — captained by Luca from <strong>Il Gavitello di Casaul</strong> [<a href="https://ilgavitello.com/en/cinque-terre-sunset-tour/">https://ilgavitello.com/en/cinque-terre-sunset-tour/</a>]. saw all five villages from sea, plus hidden coastal sections inaccessible by land. train back to La Spezia. drove back to hotel.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Italy — tips</strong></h2>

<p>1. <strong>Cinque Terre: park in La Spezia.</strong> drive to La Spezia, park there, and take train between villages.<br>2. <strong>missed a toll? don't panic.</strong> if you miss a payment — find toll operator's contact on booth, gate, or slip and email them. they will send a payment link.<br>3. book attraction tickets <strong>in advance via Tiqets</strong> [<a href="https://www.tiqets.com">https://www.tiqets.com</a>].</p>
