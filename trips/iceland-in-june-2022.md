<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 0 · Mon, Jun 6</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Seattle [SEA] → Keflavík [KEF]</p>
</blockquote>

<p>departed <strong>SEA</strong> at 7pm on Icelandair FI682.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 1 · Tue, Jun 7</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>KEF → Selfoss → Kerið → Gullfoss → Haifoss → Hjalparfoss → Vík</p>
</blockquote>

<p>landed at <strong>KEF</strong> around 10am. picked up rental car — a white Nissan Qashqai — from <a href="https://www.bluecarrental.is/">Blue Car Rental</a> [<a href="https://www.bluecarrental.is/">https://www.bluecarrental.is/</a>]. experience was OK — found Lava Car Rental [<a href="https://www.lavacarrental.is/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">https://www.lavacarrental.is/</a>] better on a later trip in 2025. on road by 11am. drove east toward <strong>Vík</strong>. quick stop in <strong>Selfoss</strong> for a shawarma wrap at Kurdo Kebab. on the way — visited <strong>Kerið</strong> crater (formed around 6,500 years ago), <strong>Gullfoss</strong> (crowded but worth it), <strong>Haifoss</strong> (much less crowded but spectacular), and <strong>Hjalparfoss</strong> (unique twin waterfall). checked into Hotel Vík í Mýrdal by 10pm. dinner at nearby Strondin Pub — burger and ginger beer. later drove to <strong>Reynisfjara</strong> for golden hour at 11:30pm — sky never fully darkened. magic of Icelandic summer.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Hotel Vík í Mýrdal [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Vik-I-Myrdal-Hotels-Hotel-Vik.h35767873.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Vik-I-Myrdal-Hotels-Hotel-Vik.h35767873.Hotel-Information</a>] — good clean room with great breakfast. 4/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 2 · Wed, Jun 8</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Vík → Skógafoss → Seljalandsfoss → Dyrhólaey → Þakgil → Eldhraun → Fjaðrárgljúfur → Kirkjubæjarklaustur</p>
</blockquote>

<p>went out early around 6am. drove to <strong>Skógafoss</strong> — my favorite waterfall in Iceland. then to <strong>Seljalandsfoss</strong> — the waterfall you can walk behind. early morning is the right time to experience Iceland in summer — before crowds arrive. back to hotel for breakfast and checked out. explored Vík and <strong>Víkurkirkja</strong> — iconic white church on hilltop above town. drove to <strong>Dyrhólaey</strong> — spotted many <strong>puffins</strong> here, funny-looking cute colorful birds. later drove to <strong>Katla Geopark</strong> — <strong>Þakgil</strong>, a hidden valley campsite nestled in highlands — a distinctly Icelandic landscape, vibrant, mossy and wild. Katla is one of Iceland's most active volcanoes, lying beneath Mýrdalsjökull glacier. hiked around Þakgil for a while — geopark has many hiking routes and rugged trails to explore. then drove to <strong>Eldhraun</strong> — a vast lava field, now blanketed in thick green moss. visited <strong>Fjaðrárgljúfur</strong> canyon — around 100m deep with sheer walls and a serpentine shape, formed at the end of last Ice Age. checked into Magma Hotel by 3:30pm. dinner at nearby Systrakaffi — chicken and ginger beer. went to bed early — needed rest for a midnight hike.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Magma Hotel [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Kirkjubaejarklaustur-Hotels-Magma-Hotel.h19532793.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Kirkjubaejarklaustur-Hotels-Magma-Hotel.h19532793.Hotel-Information</a>] — individual cabin-style rooms in a stunning lakeside setting with good views. excellent restaurant. 5/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 3 · Thu, Jun 9</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Kirkjubæjarklaustur → Skaftafell → Fjallsárlón → Jökulsárlón → Höfn</p>
</blockquote>

<p>started in the middle of night — around 1:30am. outside was lit up like dawn — effect of midnight sun. drove east. parked at <strong>Skaftafell</strong> campground parking and started hiking. passed <strong>Hundafoss</strong> and reached <strong>Svartifoss</strong> — a nice 1.5-hour hike in complete peace, no one on the trail. on way back — photographed reflection of <strong>Lómagnúpur</strong> mountain, a popular shot from roadside. back to hotel and checked out after a good breakfast. drove east for a scheduled glacier walk. Mountain Guides cancelled "3.5-hour Skaftafell Glacier Hike" [<a href="https://www.mountainguides.is/tour/blue-ice-glacier-walk">https://www.mountainguides.is/tour/blue-ice-glacier-walk</a>] the previous day — quickly found a replacement and booked "5-Hour Skaftafell Adventure Glacier Hike" [<a href="https://troll.is/tour/skaftafell-5-hour-glacier-hike/">https://troll.is/tour/skaftafell-5-hour-glacier-hike/</a>] with Troll Expeditions instead, 10:30am–3:30pm. safety briefing, gear up, then hiked up the glacier. a great experience. later drove to <strong>Fjallsárlón</strong> — walked around. took iceberg boat tour at 5:30pm — they provided protective gear against water splash and freezing wind. glacial blue ice, twice in a day — on foot earlier, now drifting past. drove to <strong>Jökulsárlón</strong>. walked around and explored <strong>Diamond Beach </strong>— pretty crowded. later checked into Hrafnavellir Guest House around 9pm. drove to <strong>Höfn</strong> for dinner at Pakkhús — lamb and ginger beer.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Hrafnavellir Guest House [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Hofn-Hotels-Hrafnavellir-Guest-House.h27781533.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Hofn-Hotels-Hrafnavellir-Guest-House.h27781533.Hotel-Information</a>] — cute room with very friendly owners and awesome breakfast. great location. 5/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 4 · Fri, Jun 10</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Höfn → Vestrahorn → Seyðisfjörður → Mývatn</p>
</blockquote>

<p>started around 6:30am. drove to <strong>Vestrahorn</strong> — main reason I came to Höfn. unfortunately heavy rain and low-hanging cloud made the mountain completely invisible. drove back to hotel, had great breakfast and checked out. plan was to drive east along coast — but weather was harsh, heavy rain and strong wind at 2°C. drove inland toward northeast instead. crossed <strong>Folaldafoss</strong> and countless more waterfalls along the way. stopped and hiked at <strong>Fardagafoss</strong> — short hike. visited <strong>Seyðisfjörður</strong> — a charming fjord town known for its colorful wooden houses and rainbow-painted street leading to its blue church. also ferry terminal for Iceland to Denmark, via Faroe Islands. the road into town crosses Fjarðarheiði mountain pass — one of the highest paved roads in Iceland, with heavy snow even in June. visited turf sheephouses at <strong>Hjarðarhagi</strong> farm — free to visit roadside. stopped at <strong>Námafjall</strong> geothermal area — air heavy with stink of sulphur. checked into Icelandair Hotel Mývatn at 4:30pm — nice view of town church from room. lazily drove around <strong>Lake Mývatn</strong>. dinner at Eldey Restaurant — lamb chop and ginger beer. visited <strong>Skútustaðagígar</strong> pseudocraters — a protected natural monument, personally didn't find it interesting. visited <strong>Hverfjall</strong> — a large tephra ring volcano crater, about 1km wide.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Icelandair Hotel Mývatn [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Myvatn-Hotels-Icelandair-Hotel-Myvatn.h20078082.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Myvatn-Hotels-Icelandair-Hotel-Myvatn.h20078082.Hotel-Information</a>] — nice modern hotel with very friendly staff. outdoor hot tubs — great. 5/5</p>
</blockquote>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 5 · Sat, Jun 11</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Mývatn → Húsavík → Krafla → Mývatn</p>
</blockquote>

<p>drove to <strong>Húsavík</strong> in the morning — a charming fishing town, known as whale watching capital. also one of Iceland's oldest settlements. walked the marina area — pretty crowded with many tourists for different boat tours. took whale and puffin boat tour [<a href="https://www.northsailing.is/tour/whale-watching-and-puffins/">https://www.northsailing.is/tour/whale-watching-and-puffins/</a>] with <a href="https://www.northsailing.is/tour/whale-watching-and-puffins/">North Sailing</a> at 9:00am — weather was pretty foggy and wet, didn't get to see much. drove back and visited <strong>Krafla</strong> area. hiked <strong>Leirhnjúkur</strong> — a lava field — for an hour. entire area was covered with low-hanging cloud - had a hard time following trail markers. back to hotel by 6:00pm. dinner at Vogafjós Cowshed Café — arctic char. great food, known for farm-to-table food — reservation recommended. a light day to recover and rest.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">day 6 · Sun, Jun 12</h3>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Mývatn → Goðafoss → Akureyri → Tröllaskagi → Siglufjörður → Sauðanesviti → Hofsós → Skagafjörður</p>
</blockquote>

<p>checked out around 7:30am. drove by <strong>Goðafoss</strong> — an iconic waterfall. by this point, starting to develop Icelandic waterfall sickness — "hmmm, yet another fall." reached <strong>Akureyri</strong> by 9am — capital of north Iceland, second largest city. visited <strong>Akureyrarkirkja</strong> and walked around downtown. coffee at Kristjáns Bakarí. drove north to <strong>Siglufjörður</strong> — a charming fishing town with colorful wooden houses, just below the Arctic Circle. stopped by <strong>Sauðanesviti</strong> — a bright orange lighthouse on the dramatic northern coastline. drove to <strong>Hofsós</strong> — too early to check in, explored the area. checked into Sóti Lodge around 4pm. sole guest that day — got undivided attention from housekeeper and personal chef. an unexpected 4-course dinner — outstanding. room had a wide open vista of Arctic Ocean and vibrant purple lupin in bloom. after dinner, drove around the area — first encounter with Arctic Terns' aggressive divebombing. back to lodge, watched midnight sun from bed — sun moved along the horizon, never fully set, just dipped and rose again.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Sóti Lodge [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Skagafjorour-Hotels-Soti-Lodge.h45578356.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Skagafjorour-Hotels-Soti-Lodge.h45578356.Hotel-Information</a>] — stunning location with panoramic views from every angle. room had breathtaking views of open ocean and purple lupin. personal chef was an unexpected and fantastic touch. housekeeper was attentive and always available. best stay of this trip. 5/5.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">summer Iceland — tips</h2>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>magic of summer light.</strong> daylight never fully ends in June — sky stays bright even at midnight. take advantage of long days: hike at 2am. blue and golden hours stretch on forever — plenty of time to compose the perfect shot.</li>

<li><strong>waterfall sickness is real.</strong> after a few days, Iceland's endless waterfalls may leave you numb to it. pick the ones that matter to you — but you don't have to stop for every one.</li>

<li><strong>go early — before the crowds.</strong> popular sites fill up by mid-morning. arriving at 6–7am is a completely different experience.</li>

<li><strong>lupine blooms everywhere.</strong> vivid purple lupine carpets roadsides, hills and valleys — perfect foreground for landscape shots.</li>

<li><strong>watch out for Arctic Terns.</strong> they nest and lay eggs directly on the ground — no trees in Iceland. stray too close and they will divebomb you. keep your distance.</li>

<li><strong>F-roads open late.</strong> Landmannalaugar (F225) and most highland roads may not open until late June or early July. plan accordingly.</li>
</ol>
