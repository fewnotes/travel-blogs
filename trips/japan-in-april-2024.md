<h2 class="wp-block-heading">day 0 · Thu, Apr 4</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>&nbsp;Vancouver [YVR] → Tokyo [NRT]</p>
</blockquote>

<p>departed YVR at 1:50pm on American Airlines AA8435, operated by Japan Airlines. 10h 40m flight. overnight to Narita.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 1 · Fri, Apr 5</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>&nbsp;Narita → Shibuya</p>
</blockquote>

<p>landed at Narita at 4:30pm. met JR Pass Meet &amp; Greet team at arrivals — picked up 14-day unlimited rail pass at JR counter. picked up NinjaWifi pocket WiFi router for unlimited data across Japan. took Narita Express (N'EX) to <strong>Shibuya</strong> — first encounter with <strong>Tokyo</strong>'s train system. messed up a connection and took longer than expected. arrived Shibuya around 9pm. checked into hotel - Shibuya Stream Excel Hotel — got a room with a view of famous <strong>Shibuya Crossing</strong>. late dinner at Great Burger Stand around 10pm — quick and good food after a long travel day.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Shibuya Stream Excel Hotel [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Tokyo-Hotels-SHIBUYA-STREAM-EXCEL-HOTEL-TOKYU.h28858266.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Tokyo-Hotels-SHIBUYA-STREAM-EXCEL-HOTEL-TOKYU.h28858266.Hotel-Information</a>] — hotel occupies upper floors of Shibuya Stream, a multi-floor commercial complex with restaurants and shops on lower floors. directly connected to Shibuya Station. got a room with a view of famous Shibuya Crossing.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 2 · Sat, Apr 6</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>&nbsp;Shibuya · Harajuku · Shinjuku · Akihabara · Odaiba</p>
</blockquote>

<p>walked to Shibuya Crossing at 8am — almost empty, completely different from any other time of day — Tokyo still not up yet. breakfast at Excelsior Caffè. took JR Yamanote Line to <strong>Harajuku</strong> at 9am. visited <strong>Meiji Jingu</strong> — a Shinto shrine founded in 1920, dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken, set in a large forested complex. walked for about an hour — peaceful. </p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p><strong>Tokyo transport</strong> — best way to get around is JR Yamanote Line (Tokyo Loop Line) [<a href="https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/yamanote-line">https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/yamanote-line</a>] — covered by JR Pass.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>took train to <strong>Shinjuku</strong> around 10am. walked around — tried <em>Dango</em> (sweet rice dumplings on a skewer) from a street stall. visited <strong>Hanazono Shrine</strong> — a 17th century Shinto shrine, tucked quietly between Shinjuku's skyscrapers. train to <strong>Akihabara</strong> around noon. had <em>Japanese chicken curry</em> for lunch. explored Akihabara <strong>Electric Town</strong> — Tokyo's famous electronics and anime district, packed with multi-floor shops selling everything from vintage electronics to manga and figurines. shopped at <em>Animate Akihabara</em>. took train back to Tokyo Station at 3pm and walked to <strong>Tokyo Imperial Palace</strong> — built on the site of historic <strong>Edo Castle</strong>, originally built in 1457. castle was closed — walked the gardens and around the moat. took train from Yurakucho back to Shibuya — Shibuya Crossing completely transformed from morning, flooded with people. later around 8pm, took train to <strong>Odaiba</strong> — visited the life-sized <strong>Unicorn Gundam statue</strong> in front of DiverCity Tokyo Plaza. dinner at food court inside DiverCity — <em>yakisoba</em> and <em>okonomiyaki</em>. walked to observation deck at Odaiba Seaside Park — wide open views over Tokyo Bay. took train back to hotel.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 3 · Sun, Apr 7</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>&nbsp;Shibuya · Shimoyoshida - Mt. Fuji  · Harajuku</p>
</blockquote>

<p>took train from Shibuya to Shinjuku at 8:30am. picked up <em>Ekiben</em> (train bento boxes) from Shinjuku Station — first of many. boarded <strong>Fuji Excursion limited express</strong> from Shinjuku. reached <strong>Shimoyoshida</strong> Station around 11am. walked ~20 minutes to <strong>Arakurayama Sengen Park</strong> and climbed 398 steps up to <strong>Chureito Pagoda</strong> — a five-storied pagoda built as a peace memorial. one of Japan's most iconic viewpoints — pagoda in the foreground, Mt Fuji rising behind. unfortunately Mt Fuji was covered in dense cloud. on the way back down — stumbled upon a samurai street play. back to station by 1:30pm. connected through Otsuki back to Shinjuku, then Harajuku — arrived around 4:30pm. walked through <strong>Takeshita Street </strong>— bumping into countless people, one of Tokyo's most famous and chaotic youth fashion streets. shopped at Warhammer store and Baseyard Tokyo for Jujutsu Kaisen merchandise. coffee break at Starbucks. walked back to hotel. dinner at Izobachi Miso Ramen around 9pm — <em>miso ramen</em>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 4 · Mon, Apr 8</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Tokyo → Osaka</p>
</blockquote>

<p>checked out at 8:30am. coffee at Starbucks. train from Shibuya to <strong>Shinagawa</strong>. boarded <strong>Hikari Shinkansen</strong> (bullet train) around noon — hit 284km/h at peak but smooth and quiet. note: Google Maps suggests taking <strong>Nozomi </strong>Shinkansen from Tokyo to <strong>Osaka</strong> — but Nozomi is not covered by JR Pass. take Hikari instead [<a href="https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/tokyo-to-osaka">https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/tokyo-to-osaka</a>]</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p><strong>Osaka transport</strong> — use Osaka Loop Line [<a href="https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/osaka-city-loop-line">https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/osaka-city-loop-line</a>] — covered by JR Pass.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>reached <strong>Shin-Osaka</strong> by 3pm. took JR Tokaido Sanyo Line — one stop to Osaka Station and walked to hotel. checked into Hotel Intergate Osaka Umeda — awesome panoramic views from corner room. later took train to <strong>Dotonbori</strong> — walked around Osaka's most famous entertainment district, bright neon signs, street food stalls, canal lined with restaurants. had <em>Korean pork BBQ</em> for dinner around 9pm.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Hotel Intergate Osaka Umeda [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Osaka-Hotels-Hotel-Intergate-Osaka-Umeda.h60867496.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Osaka-Hotels-Hotel-Intergate-Osaka-Umeda.h60867496.Hotel-Information</a>] — great location — close to Osaka Station on Osaka Loop Line. panoramic views from corner room.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 5 · Tue, Apr 9</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Osaka Castle · Namba · Shinsekai</p>
</blockquote>

<p>took train from Osaka Station to <strong>Osakajokoen</strong> Station. walked to <strong>Osaka Castle</strong> — one of Japan's most famous landmarks, originally built in 1583 by <strong>Toyotomi Hideyoshi</strong> (1537–1598), one of Japan's most powerful feudal lords who unified Japan after a century of civil war. walked around the castle grounds for a couple of hours. coffee break at Tully's Coffee inside the castle souvenir shop area. visited nearby <strong>Hokoku Shrine</strong> — a Shinto shrine founded in 1879, dedicated to Toyotomi Hideyoshi. tried <em>Takoyaki</em> — fried octopus balls — from a nearby stall. took train to <strong>Namba</strong> around 1:30pm. visited <strong>Namba Yasaka Jinja</strong> — a striking shrine famous for its giant lion-head stage. walked to <strong>Shinsaibashi</strong> covered shopping arcade. continued to <strong>Hozenji Temple</strong> — a small Buddhist temple founded in 1637, tucked in a narrow cobblestone alley between Shinsaibashi and Dotonbori. famous for its moss-covered statue of Fudo Myoo, a Buddhist deity — visitors splash water on it for good luck. walked along Dotonbori canal, tried <em>Ikayaki</em> — grilled squid on a stick — from a riverside stall. took train from Namba to <strong>Ebisuchō</strong> around 8:30pm. visited <strong>Tsutenkaku Tower</strong> — iconic tower in <strong>Shinsekai</strong>, Osaka's retro entertainment district. McDonald's nearby. train back to hotel.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 7 · Thu, Apr 11</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>&nbsp;Osaka → Kyoto</p>
</blockquote>

<p>took Osaka Loop Line to Shitenno-ji-Yuhigaoka Station around 9:45am. walked to <strong>Shitenno-ji Temple</strong> — one of Japan's oldest Buddhist temples, founded in 593 CE. later back to hotel by 11am. checked out. took JR Tokaido Sanyo Line to Kyoto Station — arrived around noon.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p><strong>Kyoto transport</strong> — trains don't cover all areas [<a href="https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/getting-around-kyoto">https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/getting-around-kyoto</a>]. buses cover most but can be confusing. taxi/Uber is convenient.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>dropped luggage at Hotel Daiwa Roynet Kyoto Ekimae Premier. lunch at Burger King near hotel. taxi to <strong>Kyoto Handicraft Centre</strong> — five floors of traditional Kyoto crafts, ceramics, textiles and souvenirs. walked 5 minutes to <strong>Heian-jingu Shrine</strong> — a grand Shinto shrine built in 1895. back to hotel and checked in. dinner at Saikatei — close to hotel — gyoza, extreme spicy ramen and dan dan noodles — tongue burned but enjoyed.</p>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Daiwa Roynet Hotel Kyoto Ekimae Premier [<a href="https://www.expedia.com/Kyoto-Hotels-Daiwa-Roynet-Hotel-Kyoto-Ekimae.h15120209.Hotel-Information">https://www.expedia.com/Kyoto-Hotels-Daiwa-Roynet-Hotel-Kyoto-Ekimae.h15120209.Hotel-Information</a>] — great location — close to restaurants, taxi stand and Kyoto Station.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp;day 8 · Fri, Apr 12</h2>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Arashiyama · Gion</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Japan — tips</h2>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>buy JR Pass well in advance</strong> — allows time for FedEx/UPS delivery. <em>Meet &amp; Greet service</em> is worth it for first time in Japan.</li>

<li><strong>get mobile data</strong> — pocket WiFi router. we used <strong>NinjaWifi</strong> (booked it with JR Pass), picked up at NRT.</li>

<li><strong>get an IC card</strong> — prepaid transit card. also accepted at convenience stores and vending machines. <strong>get one per person</strong>. we used <strong>Suica</strong> — add to Apple Wallet, top up directly from iPhone anytime, tap to pay seamlessly.</li>

<li><strong>start early</strong> — most temples and shrines are best before 9am. less crowded - peaceful and better light for photos.</li>

<li><strong>use Google Maps</strong> — works perfectly in Japan for transport and walking navigation.</li>

<li><strong>don't tip</strong> — tipping is not customary in Japan and can be considered rude.</li>

<li>book tickets and experiences <strong>in advance via Klook</strong> [<a href="https://www.klook.com/en-US/">https://www.klook.com/en-US/</a>]. we booked USJ passes and Ninja Cafe through Klook.</li>

<li><strong>eVISA</strong> (if required) — apply at evisa.mofa.go.jp [<a href="https://www.evisa.mofa.go.jp/personal/login">https://www.evisa.mofa.go.jp/personal/login</a>].</li>

<li>Japanese dishes to try outside <strong>ramen</strong>, <strong>sushi</strong>, <strong>sashimi</strong> and <strong>Tempura</strong> -
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Okonomiyaki</strong> — savoury pancake</li>

<li><strong>Soba</strong> — thin buckwheat noodles</li>

<li><strong>Yakisoba</strong> — stir-fried wheat noodles</li>

<li><strong>Udon</strong> — thick noodle</li>

<li><strong>Yaki Udon</strong> — stir-fried thick noodle</li>

<li><strong>Ekiben</strong> — lunch box for a train journey</li>

<li><strong>Wagyu beef</strong> — premium Japanese beef, known for intense marbling</li>

<li><strong>Tonkatsu</strong> — deep-fried breaded pork cutlet</li>

<li><strong>Chicken Katsu</strong> — chicken cutlet</li>

<li><strong>Gyukatsu</strong> — beef cutlet</li>

<li><strong>Yakitori</strong> — skewered grilled chicken</li>

<li><strong>Takoyaki</strong> — fried octopus balls</li>

<li><strong>Dango</strong> — sweet rice dumplings on a skewer</li>

<li><strong>Ikayaki</strong> — skewered grilled squid</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
